REGIONAL HISTORY – BELLMONT, HIGHMARK CITY, HARBOR GLEN

STATE: BELLMONT

The name Bellmont dates back to the early 1800s, when French-American surveyors first mapped the region. One of the lead explorers, Étienne Bellmont, kept detailed journals describing the area’s rolling hills and fertile valleys.

In his writings, he referred to the land as “les beaux monts” (“the beautiful hills”). Over time, English-speaking settlers merged the phrase with his surname, and the territory became known simply as Bellmont.

By the mid-1800s, the name stuck through official land charters and became associated with reliable farmland, mineral resources, and steady expansion. Bellmont developed a reputation for quiet prosperity rather than boom-and-bust growth.

CAPITAL: HIGHMARK CITY

Highmark City was founded in the 1840s on the highest natural plateau in the region. Early planners deliberately chose this location for both practical and symbolic reasons.

Surveyors placed a tall iron marker into the stone to establish a geographic reference point for land measurement. Locals began referring to the area as “the high mark,” both because it was the highest surveyed point and because it represented a place of law, order, and ambition.

When the capital was officially incorporated, it became known as Highmark City. The name reflects authority, stability, and the idea of setting a high standard for the state.

By 1990, Highmark City is seen as a firm but fair administrative center—clean, structured, and forward-looking.

TOWN: HARBOR GLEN

Harbor Glen earned its name from its role rather than its geography. In the late 1800s, expanding railroads converged in a natural valley, or “glen,” within the region.

This location became a key meeting point for multiple rail lines, and later, major trucking routes followed the same path. Merchants and rail workers began referring to the town as a “harbor,” not for ships, but because it served as a safe place to stop, trade, and regroup.

Goods from across the state would arrive, be sorted, and then sent back out, making it an essential hub of movement and exchange.

The name Harbor Glen reflects both aspects of its identity:
“Harbor” – a place of rest, trade, and connection
“Glen” – the natural landscape that made it possible

REGIONAL IDENTITY BY 1990

Bellmont is a state defined by steady growth and natural resources.
Highmark City represents leadership, structure, and progress.
Harbor Glen serves as a thriving trade hub where tradition and modern technology meet.

Together, they form a region built on history, connection, and forward momentum.
